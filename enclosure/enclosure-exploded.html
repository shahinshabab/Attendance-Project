    How it follows your sketch

    • Front, top to bottom: the 3.5" display, the sensor opening just below it, then the logo area. The display is screwed to four bosses inside the front wall, and a 14-wire jumper cable runs to the board.
    • Sensor: it stands upright at the bottom front of the case, its base 9 mm above the floor so its cable plug fits underneath. Its slanted face (window) points up and toward the user at 45°, and shows through the opening. Side walls of a small pocket in the front shell hold it and guide the finger.
    • The logo sits on the front wall in front of the sensor's lower body. It can be embossed, engraved or a sticker in a 0.5 mm recess.
    • Behind: the PCB fills the back of the case with its components on the front side, facing the display. Only the battery (upper half) and the RTC coin cell (lower half) are on the back, as in your back view.

    How it mounts and opens

    1. The wall plate is screwed to the wall first (two screws, one through a keyhole so it can be levelled).
    2. The case hangs on two hooks at the top of the plate and is locked by one security screw through the bottom wall into the plate's lower tab.
    3. Four case screws go in from the back, through posts in the back cover and the board's M3 holes, into heat-set inserts in the front shell. The wall plate hides them.
    4. To open: remove the bottom screw, lift the case off, remove the four back screws and lift the back cover away (unplug the battery). Unplug the display and sensor cables, and the board lifts out. The display and the sensor stay in the front shell.
    5. The tamper switch sits on the back cover with its plunger on the wall plate, so taking the case off the wall or opening it both trigger it.

    Board changes this layout needs

    • All components move to the front side of the board. JLCPCB then assembles the front instead of the back, still one side. Only the coin-cell holder, the battery connector J3 and the tamper connector J7 stay on the back (hand-soldered, or a second assembly side).
    • Keep the area behind the sensor empty. A 30 × 48 mm patch at the lower middle (x 20 to 50, y 100 to 147) is covered by the sensor, so no parts can go there. The barrel jack, the two 470 µF capacitors, the buck regulator and its inductor move to the left and right of it. Positions in the model are only indicative; the layout has to be redone in KiCad.
    • Board grows to 70 × 150 mm so it fills the back, and the barrel jack and USB-C stay at the bottom edge.
    • The ESP32 antenna is behind the display. The 3.5" module's metal frame sits about 15 mm in front of it and may weaken Wi-Fi. Options: let the antenna stick out past a side edge, or leave a gap above the display. Worth a signal test with the real parts.
    • J5 becomes a 14-pin header for the 3.5" module.
    • Good side effects: the status LEDs and the buzzer now face the user. The LEDs can shine through two small holes left of the sensor with short light pipes, and the case gets 3 mm thinner.
